## Q1. What do the Deobandi / South Asian Hanafi darul iftas say about listening to Quran while reading, studying or working?

### Takeaway
The main Deobandi iftas are **restrictive when attention is lost**:
- **Darul Uloom Deoband** (fatwa 150451) says not to read a religious book or do any work while listening to the Quran.
- **Banuri Town** calls background recitation without attention "against the adab of the Quran" and advises switching it off when work begins.
- **AskImam / Jamiat KZN** (under Mufti Ebrahim Desai) calls it impermissible while studying.
- **Darul Ifta Birmingham** permits it only if one is actually listening quietly and attentively.

The softer voices are:
- an older Deoband fatwa (655/550=D/1430), which calls it "allowable";
- Banuri's 2018 fatwa, which allows "gunjaish" (leeway) if one keeps attention;
- DarulIftaa.us, which allows it alongside light routine chores (driving, cooking, cleaning) but not studying.

Most sources frame the issue as **adab, or makruh / "should avoid"**. Only a few use the word "impermissible", and none I found states outright that the listener commits a major sin.

### Inferences
- The decisive factor in every source is **istimāʿ / inṣāt** (attentive, silent listening). Many of them link it to 7:204. The fatwas do not treat "work" as a single category:
  - **Mentally absorbing tasks** (reading a book, studying, writing, conversation) are where the restrictive verdicts concentrate: Deoband 150451, Jamiat/Desai, DarulIftaa.us 806, and Birmingham unless one is actually attentive.
  - **Light manual tasks** (cooking, driving, cleaning) get more leeway: DarulIftaa.us 806, Banuri 2018, Salejee, and Deoband 655/550=D/1430.
- For the user's exact scenario (reading a book while recitation plays), the closest fatwas are Deoband 150451, Jamiat/Desai and DarulIftaa.us 806. All three say not to do it. Birmingham allows it only if one genuinely listens, which while reading is practically hard.
- Even the restrictive fatwas phrase the ruling as adab, "should avoid", or "impermissible". None I found says the listener of a self-played recording commits a sin equal to ignoring live recitation in salah. The categorization as makruh tanzihi appeared only in secondary Bengali summaries.

## Q2. Is listening to a recording wajib like live recitation? The Hanafi live vs recorded distinction

### Takeaway
Deobandi iftas hold that a recording is **not legally "tilawah"**, so sajdah tilawah is not wajib when hearing a sajdah verse from a recording. Banuri states explicitly that a mobile or cassette recitation "does not have the ruling of live recitation". Even so, they still require adab: listen to it if you play it, or switch it off. A **live broadcast** (a person reciting at that moment, relayed by radio, FM or satellite) is treated like live recitation for sajdah by Deoband and others.

### Cited Findings
- **Banuri Town, fatwa 144612100920 (17 Jun 2025): "ریکارڈ شدہ تلاوت یا موبائل میں آیتِ سجدہ سننے سے سجدۂ تلاوت کاحکم".** Hearing a recorded sajdah verse, whether from mobile, radio or another device, does not make sajdah wajib. The reason given is that a recording does not have the ruling of hearing a live recitation by a conscious, valid reciter. — [banuri.edu.pk](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/rikaard-shuda-tilaawat-ya-mobile-mein-aayat-e-sajdah-sunne-se-sajda-e-tilaawat-ka-hukm-144612100920/17-06-2025)
- **Banuri Town, fatwa 144001200183 (21 Sep 2018): "ریکارڈ شدہ تلاوت سننے سے ثواب ملے گا جب کہ سجدہ تلاوت واجب نہیں ہوتا"** (listening to a recording earns reward, though sajdah tilawah is not wajib). Per the search summary, recorded recitation has the same etiquette as live recitation. — [banuri.edu.pk](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/144001200183/21-09-2018)
- **Banuri Town, fatwa 143907200061 (2018).** "موبائل یا کیسٹ کی تلاوت کا حکم براہِ راست تلاوت والا نہیں" (the ruling for mobile or cassette recitation is not that of live recitation). However, adab requires that it be listened to. — [banuri.edu.pk](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/143907200061/30-03-2018)
- **Banuri Town, fatwa 144504102283 (8 Nov 2023): "ریکارڈ شدہ تلاوت سننے پر ثواب کا حکم"** (reward for listening to recorded recitation). This confirms that reward is earned. — [banuri.edu.pk](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/144504102283/08-11-2023)
- **Banuri, Bayyinat article "آن لائن تلاوت کی صورت میں سجدۂ تلاوت کا حکم"** (sajdah tilawah for online recitation). It deals with the online/live case. — [banuri.edu.pk bayyinat](https://banuri.edu.pk/bayyinat-detail/%D8%A7-%D9%86-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A6%D9%86-%D8%AA%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%88%D8%AA-%DA%A9%DB%8C-%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%B1%D8%AA-%D9%85%DB%8C%DA%BA-%D8%B3%D8%AC%D8%AF%DB%81-%D8%AA%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%88%D8%AA-%DA%A9%D8%A7-%D8%AD%DA%A9%D9%85)
- **Darul Uloom Deoband (IslamQA mirror #111009).** Excerpt: "If you listen the Quran from CD by playing it and no person is reciting the Holy Quran physically then in such a case no sajdah tilawat will be wajib. But if someone is reciting and at the same time it is recorded in the CD then sajdah tilawat shall be wajib." — [IslamQA mirror](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/darulifta-deoband/111009/do-i-need-to-make-sajdah-when-hearing-a-quranic-verse-from-a-cd-player-with-sajdah-tilawat/)
- **AskImam (IslamQA mirror #80432).**
  - Sajdah is not compulsory on hearing a sajdah verse over a CD or mobile.
  - The related reasoning on another Hanafi mirror is that "the machine or sound instrument by which you hear the ayah from does not hold the status of a human being".
  - Live FM or satellite broadcasts are distinguished: "Sajdah Tilāwah will be performed when listening to a live recitation via FM, satellite, etc."
  - — [IslamQA askimam](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/80432/is-sajdah-tilawah-compulsory-upon-a-person-who-hears-the-ayah-of-sajdah-over-the-cd-mobile/); [MuftiSays #10272](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/muftisays/10272/should-one-do-sajdah-upon-hearing-sajdah-talawat-on-a-tape-or-cd/)
- **Contrasting non-Hanafi framing (for context only).** IslamOnline says attentive listening outside salah and the khutbah is only Sunnah, so working while it plays is fine — [fiqh.islamonline.net](https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/listening-to-the-quran-while-attending-to-other-tasks). The Salafi IslamQA.info answers 174743 and 88728 discuss whether listening outside salah is obligatory and report that the majority view is that it is not — [islamqa.info/en/answers/174743](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/174743). By contrast, the Hanafi fatwas above (AskImam #16899, Birmingham, Qibla) speak of listening as **compulsory or commanded** when recitation occurs.

### Inferences
- How Hanafis apply the distinction:
  - **Recording**: not legal tilawah, so no wajib sajdah. The strict legal "wajib istimāʿ" that attaches to live recitation (Hindiyya / Radd al-Muhtar) does not strictly attach.
  - **Adab still applies.** Hence Banuri's formula: switch it off if you won't listen. Several fatwas (AskImam #16899, Birmingham, DarulIftaa.us) nonetheless apply the 7:204 duty to playback without distinguishing it. This is an internal tension the report should note: some Deobandi muftis apply 7:204 to recordings fully, others treat it as adab.
- Since Banuri says recording ≠ live recitation, failing to listen to a recording one did not choose to play is at most a breach of adab, not a sin of leaving a wajib. This is my reading; I found no fatwa stating it in those exact words.

### Gaps
- I found no Deobandi fatwa that states in so many words "listening to a recording is not wajib, only mustahab". Banuri 143907200061 comes closest.
- Deoband's position on live broadcasts was taken from the mirror excerpt only.

## Q3. Deliberately playing it vs hearing it incidentally, and who bears the sin

### Takeaway
Classical Hanafi texts, cited by modern Deobandi fatwas, place the blame on **the person who recites or plays the Quran where others are busy and cannot listen**, not on the busy person. The standard case: a man writing fiqh beside someone reciting aloud, "the sin is on the reciter" (Khulasat al-Fatawa / Abu Nasr al-Dabusi, cited in Fath al-Qadir and DarulIftaa.us 806). A person who **chooses** to play the recording, then ignores it, falls under the adab / makruh warnings in Q1. Someone who incidentally hears recitation (another person's phone, a shop speaker) while studying is not the one at fault, though tradition encourages listening if they reasonably can.

### Cited Findings
- **Khulasat al-Fatawa, quoted in Fath al-Qadir (Ibn al-Humam), Kitab al-Salah, Fasl fi al-Qira'a.** If someone is writing fiqh next to a person reading the Quran and cannot listen, "فالإثم على القارئ" (the sin is on the reciter). It extends the case to someone reciting aloud on a rooftop at night while people sleep. Listening is described as fard kifayah, and the reciter must respect it by not reciting in markets or places of distraction. — [islamweb.net Fath al-Qadir](https://islamweb.net/newlibrary/display_book.php?ID=367&bk_no=23&flag=1)
- **DarulIftaa.us 806, footnotes.** It quotes Abu Nasr al-Dabusi on the same case: one writing fiqh while a person beside him recites, unable to listen or leave, and the sin falls on the reciter. — [daruliftaa.us/fatwa/806](https://daruliftaa.us/fatwa/806/)
- **Banuri Town 144406101673.** Recitation should not be aloud or played in gatherings where people are busy and inattentive, because it becomes a cause of sin rather than reward. Responsibility is placed on whoever creates the situation. — [banuri.edu.pk](https://www.banuri.edu.pk/readquestion/144406101673/14-01-2023)
- **Islamweb fatwa 34160 (Arabic, non-Hanafi-specific).** Applies the same logic: playing Quran in a setting where others are not listening exposes them to sin. — [islamweb.com fatwa 34160](https://www.islamweb.com/ar/fatwa/34160/)
- **Jamia Inaamiyyah (alinaam.co.za).** "Is it permissible to play a recording of Surah Baqarah whilst everyone is engaged in their own work?" It appeared in search results, but I could not read the answer text. — [alinaam.co.za](https://alinaam.co.za/is-it-permissible-to-play-a-recording-of-surah-baqarah-whilst-everyone-is-engaged-in-their-own-work/)
- **Bengali summary of the Hanafi excuse for the busy.** ifatwa.info and related summaries mention that the sin of not listening is lifted from people who are occupied, to remove hardship. An excerpt also cites Imam Abu Yusuf that listening during work is permissible, though it harms concentration. — [ifatwa.info/14565](https://ifatwa.info/14565/) (only partly visible; see Q4)

### Inferences
- Applied to the user's question:
  - **(a) You yourself start the recording and then read a book.** Restrictive fatwas say you are breaching adab and should switch it off or listen: Deoband 150451, Banuri 144008200796, Jamiat/Desai, DarulIftaa.us 806.
  - **(b) Someone else plays it, or recites live nearby, while you are studying.** Per the Khulasa / Dabusi principle, the blame lies with the reciter or player, not with you. Classical texts also treat studying fiqh as a legitimate occupation that excuses the student.
  - **(c) Live recitation in a mosque or gathering.** The stronger wajib-istimāʿ discussion applies, but it is a fard kifayah per Fath al-Qadir's commentary. This last point comes from the commentary excerpt only.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the exact Fatawa Hindiyya / Radd al-Muhtar wording ("الصبي إذا قرأ… وأهله مشغولون بالعمل يعذرون") from any page in this session. The report writer should get it from the primary text if needed.
- No Deobandi fatwa I found explicitly addresses "hearing a neighbour's / shop's recording while I study".

## Q4. Bangladeshi sources (Masik Alkawsar, Markazud Dawah, Islamic Foundation, popular scholars, Bengali Q&A sites)

### Takeaway
The leading Bangladeshi Deobandi source, **Masik Alkawsar** (Markazud Dawah al-Islamia, Dhaka), published an article whose title calls it "a mistake": **"একটি ভুল কাজ : কুরআন তিলাওয়াত চালু করে অন্য কাজ করা"** (a wrong practice: starting Quran recitation and then doing other work). Bangladeshi news religion pages and fatwa sites mostly repeat this line: not proper, against adab, listen with attention or switch it off. Some call it makruh tanzihi and say there is no sin when one is busy. I found no statements from Shaikh Ahmadullah, Mufti Mizanur Rahman Sayeed, Mufti Kazi Ibrahim, Jamia Rahmania or Islamic Foundation on this exact question.

### Cited Findings
- **Masik Alkawsar, article 831 (reported in the search excerpt as February 2013): "একটি ভুল কাজ : কুরআন তিলাওয়াত চালু করে অন্য কাজ করা".**
  - Excerpt: "আল্লাহর কালাম তিলাওয়াত হচ্ছে আর আমি অন্যদিকে মনোযোগ দিব তা হয় না"
  - Gist: it cannot be that Allah's word is being recited and I turn my attention elsewhere.
  - It notes that many people play recitation on a cassette or computer and carry on with other work. Listening is a separate act of worship.
  - Its advice: once recitation starts, listen attentively. If you must hear something while working, listen to something that is not sinful.
  - Evidence: 7:204.
  - Author and exact issue number not retrieved.
  - — [alkawsar.com/bn/article/831](https://www.alkawsar.com/bn/article/831/)

### Inferences
- The Bangladeshi Qawmi / Deobandi mainstream, led by Alkawsar / Markazud Dawah and echoed by Fakihul Millat via Dhaka Mail, takes the same line as Banuri:
  - turning recitation on and then busying oneself is **wrong / against adab**;
  - attentive listening is the remedy, or else switch it off.
- No Bangladeshi source I found calls it a major sin. The strongest terms are "ভুল কাজ" (a wrong act), "ঠিক নয়" (not right), "শিষ্টাচারপরিপন্থী" (against etiquette) and makruh tanzihi. Jago News explicitly says listening silently while working is "not blameworthy".
- None of the Bangladeshi sources discuss the Khulasa principle that the sin falls on the reciter, or the recorded-vs-live sajdah distinction, in the context of this question.
