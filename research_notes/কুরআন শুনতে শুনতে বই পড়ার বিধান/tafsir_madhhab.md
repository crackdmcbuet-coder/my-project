## Q1. What did the Sahaba and Tabi'un say 7:204 refers to: salah, khutbah, or all recitation?

### Takeaway
Almost all reports from the Sahaba and Tabi'un tie the verse to **salah**: the follower behind an imam reciting aloud, or the early practice of talking in prayer. A second group adds the **Friday/'Eid khutbah**. Tabari, the main early collector, concludes that listening is **obligatory only in these two situations**. Several early authorities say in so many words that outside salah a person is "in latitude" (fi sa'a) and may even talk. Only al-Hasan al-Basri (in one report) and the Zahiris, as Razi reports them, held a universal obligation. The Makkan-revelation argument (Nahhas, Razi) and the "general wording" argument (Qurtubi, Jassas) are later readings, not the dominant Salaf view.

### Cited Findings

**Tabari, *Jami' al-Bayan*, 13/344–353 (Shakir ed.), nos. 15581–15618.** Tabari groups the reports into three opinions ("ثم اختلف أهل التأويل في الحال التي أمر الله بالاستماع لقارئ القرآن إذا قرأ والإنصات له"):
- **Opinion 1, salah behind an imam** ("ذلك حال كون المصلي في الصلاة خلف إمام يأتم به"):
  - Ibn Mas'ud: "كنا يسلم بعضنا على بعض في الصلاة... فجاء القرآن: وإذا قرئ القرآن..." (no. 15581). Ibn Mas'ud also rebuked people reciting behind the imam: "أما آن لكم أن تفقهوا! أما آن لكم أن تعقلوا؟ وإذا قرئ القرآن فاستمعوا له وأنصتوا" (no. 15584).
  - Abu Hurayra: "كانوا يتكلمون في الصلاة، فلما نزلت هذه الآية... أمروا بالإنصات" (no. 15582). Another report: "نزلت في رفع الأصوات وهم خلف رسول الله ﷺ في الصلاة" (no. 15586).
  - Ibn 'Abbas (via 'Ali b. Abi Talha): "يعني: في الصلاة المفروضة" (no. 15604).
  - Ibn 'Abbas (via Ibn Hubayra) says that what is in the obligatory prayer is binding, while "ما كان من قصص أو قراءة بعد ذلك، فإنما هي نافلة" (no. 15608).
  - Mujahid, many chains: "في الصلاة" / "في الصلاة المكتوبة" (nos. 15587–15595).
  - Sa'id b. al-Musayyab: "في الصلاة" (no. 15588).
  - Sa'id b. Jubayr, al-Dahhak, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i and al-Sha'bi ('Amir): "في الصلاة المكتوبة" (nos. 15593, 15596, 15597, 15602).
  - Qatada: people used to talk about their needs in prayer, or a latecomer would ask "كم صليتم؟ كم بقي؟" (nos. 15598–15599).
  - al-Zuhri: revealed about a young Ansari man who recited along with the Prophet ﷺ (nos. 15583, 15600).
  - al-Suddi: "إذا قرئ في الصلاة" (no. 15603).
  - Ibn Zayd: "هذا إذا قام الإمام للصلاة" (no. 15606).

  - **Sa'id b. Jubayr:** "الإنصات: يوم الأضحى، ويوم الفطر، ويوم الجمعة، وفيما يجهر به الإمام من الصلاة" (no. 15616).
  - **al-Hasan al-Basri:** "في الصلاة المكتوبة، وعند الذكر" (nos. 15614, 15617). This is the only early report that widens the scope beyond prayer and khutbah.

**Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, *Mafatih al-Ghayb*, 15/439–440.** He starts from the principle that "ظاهر الأمر للوجوب" and lists five opinions:

### Inferences
- Among the Salaf, the predominant reading limits the **obligation** to salah, and to the khutbah for many. At least three early authorities state plainly that one may talk or do other things during recitation outside salah: Ibn 'Abbas ("المؤمن في سعة"), Mujahid ("لا بأس... أن يتكلم"), and 'Ubayd b. 'Umayr with 'Ata'. That is strong evidence against treating ordinary non-attention outside salah as sinful.
- The case for a broader duty rests on the **general wording** of the verse ("العبرة لعموم اللفظ لا لخصوص السبب"; Nahhas, Qurtubi, Jassas). Later Hanafis rely on exactly this argument (see Q2).
- Within the tafsir tradition, Qurtubi is the one most open to "generality". His point, however, is that the verse is not confined to the Prophet's lifetime or to the khutbah. He does not say a person working while a recording plays is sinful.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the full Ma'arif al-Qur'an text (Urdu original or English) for 7:204; quran.com was blocked. Only search snippets were seen.
- The page numbers for Ibn Kathir apply to an unidentified edition; for the widely used Dar Tayba edition (ed. Sami Salama) I have no verified page.
- The al-Awsat volume number is not marked in the OpenITI file. The page appears to be about 3/105–106 (kitab al-salat), but this is unverified.

## Q2. Hanafi position: is listening outside salah wajib, fard kifaya, or mustahabb? Exact texts, including the rulings on reciting aloud near people who are working or sleeping

### Takeaway
The later Hanafi texts begin with an **unqualified "wajib"** ("يجب الاستماع للقراءة مطلقا", *al-Durr al-Mukhtar*), justified by the general wording of the verse. They then narrow it in two ways:
- **Excuse ('udhr):** someone who was already working when the recitation began is excused (*al-Qunya*).
- **The burden falls on the reciter:** *al-Khulasa*, cited by *Fath al-Qadir* and *al-Bahr al-Ra'iq*, says that if a man is writing fiqh and someone recites beside him so that he cannot listen, "the sin is on the reciter". The same applies to reciting aloud on a rooftop at night while people sleep.

Ibn 'Abidin then cites *Sharh al-Munya* (al-Halabi): **listening is a fard kifaya**. It is satisfied when some of those present listen, and the reciter must not recite in markets and workplaces; if he does, the sin is his. A minority view (Minqari-zade) held that listening is fard 'ayn.

### Cited Findings

**Ibn 'Abidin on the follower inside salah (same page, just before):** he quotes al-Bahr that the verse commands two things, listening and silence. Listening applies to audible prayers. Silence is unrestricted: "فيجب السكوت عند القراءة مطلقا". — [Radd al-Muhtar 1/546](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1275AH/master/data/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar.Shamela0021613-ara1)

### Inferences
Application to the user's question, per the Hanafi texts:
- **Someone already reading a book (or studying fiqh) when a recording or reciter starts** is excused. *Al-Qunya*: "يعذرون... إن افتتحوا العمل قبل القراءة", and "وكذا قراءة الفقه".
- **Where recitation is aloud in a place where people are busy**, any sin falls on the reciter, or by extension the person who plays the recording, not on the busy listener. (*Khulasa*; *Sharh al-Munya*: "الإثم عليه دون أهل الاشتغال دفعا للحرج".)
- **In a gathering**, listening is fard kifaya. If some listen, the others are not sinful.
- **The hardest case:** a person deliberately *begins* an unrelated task after a recitation has started and, as a result, nobody present is attending to it. Some Hanafi texts would count this as sinful ("وإلا أثموا"), though even this is softened by the fard kifaya analysis.
- Treating a recorded or broadcast recitation as "qira'a" is a modern extension the classical texts do not address. See the separate contemporary-fatwa notes (arab_fatwas.md and deobandi_bangladeshi.md in this folder).

## Q3. Shafi'i, Maliki and Hanbali positions: is listening outside salah mustahabb?

### Takeaway
For the three non-Hanafi schools, the **obligation** of the verse applies to the follower in salah and, for most, the khutbah. Outside salah, listening is **mustahabb** and idle talk during recitation is **makruh**, not haram:
- **Shafi'i.** Nawawi says that talking during recitation outside salah "is not forbidden by consensus".
- **Hanbali.** Buhuti says listening "is recommended" and idle talk "is disliked".
- **Maliki.** The school's tafsir authorities (Ibn al-'Arabi, Qurtubi) discuss the verse almost entirely as a salah/khutbah ruling. Qurtubi favours reading the verse as general, but I found no Maliki text declaring non-attention outside salah sinful.

### Cited Findings

**Shafi'i: Nawawi, *al-Tibyan*.**
- **Ch. on respecting the Quran (p. 47):**
  > "ومما يعتنى به ويتأكد الأمر به احترام القرآن من أمور قد يتساهل فيها بعض الغافلين القارئين مجتمعين فمن ذلك **اجتناب الضحك واللغط والحديث في خلال القراءة إلا كلاما يضطر إليه** وليمتثل قول الله تعالى: وإذا قرئ القرآن فاستمعوا له وأنصتوا لعلكم ترحمون، وليقتد بما رواه ابن أبي داود عن ابن عمر رضي الله عنهما أنه كان إذا قرأ القرآن لا يتكلم حتى يفرغ منه"

  He continues with "ومن ذلك العبث باليد... ومن ذلك **النظر إلى ما يلهي ويبدد الذهن**". Note that this is addressed to *reciters gathered together* and is framed as adab ("ينبغي/يتأكد"), not as a prohibition.
- **On greeting someone who is reciting (p. 62–63):** Nawawi says returning a greeting by speech is required even then. His reasoning: if this holds during the khutbah, where listening and talking are disputed, then "**ففي حال القراءة التي لا يحرم الكلام فيها بالإجماع أولى**" (all the more so during recitation outside salah, *in which talking is not forbidden by consensus*).

This is an explicit Shafi'i claim of consensus that talking during recitation outside salah is not haram. — [Tibyan](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0700AH/master/data/0676Nawawi/0676Nawawi.Tibyan/0676Nawawi.Tibyan.JK000125-ara1)

**Shafi'i: Nawawi, *al-Majmu'* (around 3/366–367).** He reports al-Shafi'i's old position on 7:204: "قال الشافعي في القديم هذا عندنا في القراءة التي تسمع خاصة", i.e. it applies inside salah to the audible recitation. Al-Shafi'i's new position requires the follower to recite al-Fatiha even in audible prayers, so even inside salah the Shafi'is read the verse narrowly. Ibn al-Mundhir (*al-Awsat*) gives the same old/new account of al-Shafi'i's view. — [Majmu'](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0700AH/master/data/0676Nawawi/0676Nawawi.Majmuc/0676Nawawi.Majmuc.Shamela0002186-ara1); [al-Awsat](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0325AH/master/data/0319IbnMundhirNaysaburi/0319IbnMundhirNaysaburi.AwsatFiSunan/0319IbnMundhirNaysaburi.AwsatFiSunan.Shamela0021113-ara1)

**Shafi'i: secondary source.** A search summary reports that *al-Jami' fi Ahkam Sifat al-Salah* states listening is mustahabb for the Shafi'is and talk without benefit during recitation is makruh. I did not read the page. — [kalemtayeb.com](https://kalemtayeb.com/safahat/item/103934) (search snippet only)

**Hanbali: Buhuti, *Kashshaf al-Qina'* 1/432 (Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya), commenting on *al-Iqna'*:**
> "(و) يستحب (الاستماع لها) أي للقراءة؛ لأنه يشارك القارئ في أجره. (ويكره الحديث عندها) أي القراءة (بما لا فائدة فيه) لقوله تعالى {وإذا قرئ القرآن فاستمعوا له وأنصتوا لعلكم ترحمون} ولأنه إعراض عن الاستماع الذي يترتب عليه الأجر بما لا طائل تحته."

This is the formal Hanbali madhhab position: listening is **mustahabb**, and **useless** talk during recitation is **makruh**. Implicitly, talk or activity that does have a benefit is not even disliked. — [Kashshaf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1075AH/master/data/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali.KashshafQinac/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali.KashshafQinac.Shamela0021642-ara1)

Ibn Qudama uses the verse only for the question of the follower not reciting behind the imam. — [Mughni](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0625AH/master/data/0620IbnQudamaMaqdisi/0620IbnQudamaMaqdisi.Mughni/0620IbnQudamaMaqdisi.Mughni.Shamela0008463-ara1)

> "من كان يقرأ القرآن والناس يصلون تطوعا فليس له أن يجهر جهرا يشغلهم به؛ فإن النبي ﷺ خرج على أصحابه وهم يصلون من السحر فقال: يا أيها الناس كلكم يناجي ربه فلا يجهر بعضكم على بعض في القراءة"

This parallels the Hanafi "sin is on the reciter" principle: the reciter must not disturb people who are occupied. — [Majmu' al-Fatawa](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0750AH/master/data/0728IbnTaymiyya/0728IbnTaymiyya.MajmucFatawa/0728IbnTaymiyya.MajmucFatawa.Shamela0007289-ara1)

**Maliki: Ibn al-'Arabi, *Ahkam al-Qur'an* 2/363–366.**
- He treats 7:204 as the Quranic basis for the Maliki/Madinan practice: the follower does not recite in audible prayers ("لا سبيل إلى القراءة فيه... أنه عمل أهل المدينة... أنه حكم القرآن") but does in silent ones.

### Inferences
- There is a real **disagreement** between the later Hanafi books and the other schools:
  - The later Hanafi books (Fath, Bahr, Durr, Radd) call listening outside salah wajib. In Radd al-Muhtar this is refined to **fard kifaya**, with the sin placed on the reciter in places of work.
  - Shafi'is, Hanbalis and (implicitly) Malikis hold it **mustahabb**, with useless talk makruh.
  - Tabari and Ibn al-Mundhir (and Nawawi for speech) claim consensus that there is **no** obligation outside salah and khutbah.
- **Under all four schools**, quietly reading a book or doing other work while a recitation plays nearby is **not sinful for the listener**:
  - Non-Hanafis: listening is only recommended.
  - Hanafis: the busy person is excused, or the duty is discharged by others, or the sin falls on whoever recites or plays it where people are occupied.
  - Even so, all schools treat attentive listening as the better adab ("يشارك القارئ في أجره").

### Gaps
- I did not locate an explicit Maliki fiqh-manual statement (e.g. al-Dardir/al-Dasuqi, al-Kharashi) that listening outside salah is mustahabb or that talking is makruh. The Maliki position here is inferred from the tafsir authorities and from Nawawi's and Ibn al-Mundhir's consensus claims.
- I could not check whether *al-Mawsu'a al-Fiqhiyya* (4/86, "istima'") attributes the "wajib" view specifically to the Hanafis.

## Q4. Classical discussion of a person doing another task (writing, studying, working) while hearing the Quran

### Takeaway
The classical Hanafi corpus addresses this exact scenario. *Al-Khulasa* (via *Fath al-Qadir* and *al-Bahr*) treats a man **writing fiqh** next to a reciter. *Al-Qunya* (via *al-Bahr* and *Radd*) treats a household **working** while a child recites, and extends the same ruling to **studying fiqh**. The rulings are:
1. Sin falls on the reciter who recites aloud where people are occupied.
2. Busy people are excused if they began their work before the recitation started.
3. Listening is a communal duty met by some of those present.

Non-Hanafi sources treat talking or distraction during recitation as **disliked adab**, not sin. Early authorities ('Ata', 'Ubayd b. 'Umayr, Mujahid) carried on talking during non-prayer recitation and said this was permitted.

### Cited Findings
- **Writing fiqh next to a reciter.** *Al-Khulasa*: "رجل يكتب الفقه وبجنبه رجل يقرأ القرآن فلا يمكنه استماع القرآن فالإثم على القارئ" (Fath al-Qadir 1/342; al-Bahr 1/364; Radd al-Muhtar 1/546). — [Fath al-Qadir](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0875AH/master/data/0861IbnHumam/0861IbnHumam.FathQadir/0861IbnHumam.FathQadir.Shamela0021744-ara1); [Bahr](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0975AH/master/data/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri.BahrRaiq/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri.BahrRaiq.Shamela0012227BK1-ara1)
- **Rooftop recitation at night.** "لو قرأ على السطح في الليل جهرا والناس نيام يأثم". Ibn 'Abidin gives two reasons: the reciter causes people to turn away from listening, or he harms them by waking them ("لأنه يكون سببا لإعراضهم عن استماعه، أو لأنه يؤذيهم بإيقاظهم"). — [Radd al-Muhtar 1/546](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1275AH/master/data/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar.Shamela0021613-ara1)
- **Child reciting at home while the family works.** *Al-Qunya*: "صبي يقرأ في البيت وأهله مشغولون بالعمل يعذرون في ترك الاستماع إن افتتحوا العمل قبل القراءة وإلا فلا، **وكذا قراءة الفقه عند قراءة القرآن**". *Al-Bahr*'s wording: "إن كان شرعوا في العمل قبل قراءته لا يأثمون وإلا أثموا". — [Radd](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1275AH/master/data/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar.Shamela0021613-ara1); [Bahr](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0975AH/master/data/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri.BahrRaiq/0970IbnIbrahimIbnNujaymMisri.BahrRaiq.Shamela0012227BK1-ara1)
- **Markets and workplaces.** *Sharh al-Munya*: "يجب على القارئ احترامه بأن لا يقرأه في الأسواق ومواضع الاشتغال، فإذا قرأه فيها كان هو المضيع لحرمته، فيكون الإثم عليه دون أهل الاشتغال دفعا للحرج". — [Radd 1/546–547](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1275AH/master/data/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar/1252IbnCabidinDimashqi.RaddMukhtar.Shamela0021613-ara1)
- **The reciter himself working.** Ibn al-Humam: reciting while walking, weaving or spinning "إن كان القلب حاضرا غير مشتغل لا يكره". — [Fath al-Qadir 1/342](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0875AH/master/data/0861IbnHumam/0861IbnHumam.FathQadir/0861IbnHumam.FathQadir.Shamela0021744-ara1)
- **Early practice.** 'Ubayd b. 'Umayr and 'Ata' kept talking during a storyteller's session and said "إنما ذلك في الصلاة". Mujahid: "لا بأس إذا قرأ الرجل في غير الصلاة أن يتكلم". (Tabari 13/345, 348.) — [Tabari](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0325AH/master/data/0310Tabari/0310Tabari.JamicBayan/0310Tabari.JamicBayan.Shamela0000043-ara1)
- **Shafi'i adab.** Nawawi tells people reciting together to avoid laughter, noise, talk "and looking at what distracts" during recitation ("النظر إلى ما يلهي ويبدد الذهن"). He also says talking during recitation outside salah is not haram by consensus. — [Tibyan pp. 47, 62–63](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0700AH/master/data/0676Nawawi/0676Nawawi.Tibyan/0676Nawawi.Tibyan.JK000125-ara1)
- **Hanbali.** Useless talk during recitation is makruh because it is "إعراض عن الاستماع الذي يترتب عليه الأجر بما لا طائل تحته". — [Kashshaf 1/432](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/1075AH/master/data/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali.KashshafQinac/1051IbnYunusBuhutiHanbali.KashshafQinac.Shamela0021642-ara1)
- **Not disturbing others.** Ibn Taymiyya: a reciter may not recite loudly in a way that distracts people praying voluntary prayers. — [Majmu' al-Fatawa 23/61](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenITI/0750AH/master/data/0728IbnTaymiyya/0728IbnTaymiyya.MajmucFatawa/0728IbnTaymiyya.MajmucFatawa.Shamela0007289-ara1)

### Inferences
- The classical analogue to "reading a book while Quran plays" is the *Khulasa*/*Qunya* case of writing or studying fiqh while someone recites. The Hanafi answer is that the sin, if any, lies with whoever recites aloud where people are occupied, and that a person already engaged in study is excused.
- By analogy, a person who **chooses to play** a recording and then deliberately ignores it occupies a different position from a passer-by or someone who was already busy. Under the *Sharh al-Munya* logic, that person is the one who "neglects its sanctity". Classical texts do not address recordings directly, so this is an inference.
- Across the schools, the soundest summary is:
  - Attentive listening is meritorious.
  - Deliberately turning a live recitation into background noise is poor adab (makruh for Hanbalis and Shafi'is).
  - Formal sin (ithm) is affirmed only in the later Hanafi texts, and even there it falls primarily on the reciter in places of work.

### Gaps
- I found no classical text that directly addresses mechanical or recorded recitation (radio, cassette, phone). That is a modern question; see the contemporary fatwa notes.
- I did not read the original texts of *al-Khulasa*, *al-Qunya*, *Sharh al-Munya* or al-Tahtawi's hashiya; they are known here only as quoted by later authors.
- I could not verify the Fatawa Hindiyya wording.
