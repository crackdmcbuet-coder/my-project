## Which books directly address "brain fog", and what is each one's content, credentials and approach?

### Takeaway
Few of these books use "brain fog" in the title. Dr Mike Dow's *The Brain Fog Fix* does, Dr Sabina Brennan's *Beating Brain Fog* does, and there is a long tail of low-quality self-published menopause titles. Most of the canonical titles (*Grain Brain*, *Genius Foods*, *The UltraMind Solution*, *Why Isn't My Brain Working?*, *Brain Food*, *This Is Your Brain on Food*, *The Menopause Brain*) treat brain fog as one symptom inside a wider diet, hormone or "root-cause" framework. The authors range from academic neuroscientists (Mosconi, Bullmore, Mayer) to functional-medicine practitioners (Hyman, Kharrazian) and a journalist with no science training (Lugavere).

### Inferences
- A book's "brain fog" focus does not track its quality. The books with the strongest science (Bullmore, Mayer, McKay, and Mosconi in part) rarely use the phrase. The books that use it most (Dow, the self-published menopause titles) have thin evidence.
- A sensible tiering for the final report:
  - (A) Researcher-written, cautious: Mayer, Bullmore, Cryan/Dinan, McKay, and Mosconi with caveats.
  - (B) Clinician-written, practical, mostly mainstream diet advice: Naidoo, Haver, Dow.
  - (C) Functional-medicine or ideological diet books with overclaiming or commercial ties: Perlmutter, Hyman, Kharrazian, Lugavere, Wentz.

## What do scientists and critics say about each book's evidence?

### Takeaway
*Grain Brain* draws the most criticism: it is a distorted, belief-driven reading of observational and animal data, and it has supplement ties. *Genius Foods*/Lugavere was criticised by McGill's Office for Science and Society. Hyman's work is criticised mainly through attacks on functional medicine as a whole. Kharrazian's book has no formal scientific critique, but its gluten and autoimmunity claims and supplement-heavy approach fit the same functional-medicine pattern. Mosconi, Mayer and Bullmore are seen as credible, though some of Mosconi's menopause–Alzheimer's claims are considered still emerging or overstated.

### Cited Findings
**Grain Brain / Perlmutter**
- The Whole Grains Council (an industry-aligned body) called it "a misleading and sensationalist title for a book that distorts current science." — [Whole Grains Council blog](https://wholegrainscouncil.org/print/blog/2013/09/whole-grains-and-healthy-brains)
- Nutrition experts said the advice to avoid grains is "unhelpful." — [FoodNavigator 2013](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2013/09/18/Grain-Brain-Experts-say-advice-to-avoid-grains-is-unhelpful/)
- Critics say the book relies on observational studies and animal research, cites studies selectively and leaves out the full body of evidence. They also note that its language slides between "carbohydrates" and "gluten" (Alex Ruani, Health Sciences Academy). — [Health Sciences Academy review](https://thehealthsciencesacademy.org/book-reviews/grain-brain/); [Energy Skeptic review](https://energyskeptic.com/?p=9782); [Doc's Opinion](https://www.docsopinion.com/?p=2699)
- Its Paleolithic diet argument is not supported by anthropological research. Perlmutter has recommended Protandim, a multi-level-marketing supplement from LifeVantage. ### Inferences
- Shared red flags across the tier-C books: universal elimination diets (gluten or grains), long supplement protocols often tied to the author's own products, and reasoning that extrapolates from observational or animal data. Hyman, Perlmutter, Kharrazian and Wentz are all aligned with functional medicine.
- Shared green flags in tier-A books: written by active researchers, cautious about individual-level fixes, and diet advice that ends up close to the Mediterranean or MIND pattern.
- Even tier-C books contain mainstream-compatible advice: whole foods, less ultra-processed food and sugar, sleep, exercise, stress reduction. Readers can take that part and skip the supplement stacks.

- I found no expert review of Kharrazian, Naidoo, Haver or Dow by a credible outlet such as NYT, Guardian or SBM.

## Which books are best for menopause/hormonal brain fog, thyroid, and gut issues?

### Takeaway
- **Menopause:** Mosconi's *The Menopause Brain* is the most scientifically grounded on the brain, written by a Weill Cornell neuroscientist, though some of its Alzheimer's-link claims are still emerging. Haver's *The New Menopause* is the most practical guide from a clinician. McKay's *The Women's Brain Book* (2025 revised edition) is a sober, neuroscientist-written alternative.
- **Thyroid:** the popular options (Wentz, Kharrazian) are patient-led or functional-medicine and supplement-heavy. No strongly evidence-based popular book on thyroid-related brain fog was found. The safest advice is to get tested and treated by an endocrinologist.
- **Gut-brain:** Mayer's *The Mind-Gut Connection* is the most credible, with Cryan and Dinan's *The Psychobiotic Revolution* as a researcher-written alternative.
- **Inflammation:** Bullmore's *The Inflamed Mind* is the credible science book, though it is more explanatory than prescriptive.
- **Diet for mood and brain fog generally:** Naidoo (practical, clinician-written) and Mosconi's *Brain Food*.

### Cited Findings
- Malone called *The Menopause Brain* "the gold standard" on estrogen and the female brain. It covers hormone therapy and lifestyle. — [Indigo](https://www.indigo.ca/en-ca/the-menopause-brain-new-science-empowers-women-to-navigate-the-pivotal-transition-with-knowledge-and-confidence/9780593541241.html)

### Inferences
Suggested matching of reader type to book, for the report writer:
- **Perimenopausal or menopausal woman with forgetfulness:** Mosconi's *The Menopause Brain* plus Haver's *The New Menopause*, or McKay. Skip the self-published "Menopausal Brain" titles.
- **Diagnosed Hashimoto's or hypothyroid with lingering fog:** Wentz, read with caution: use it as a symptom checklist, discuss supplements with a doctor, and avoid her expensive branded stacks.
- **IBS, digestive issues or mood problems:** Mayer, plus *The Psychobiotic Revolution*.
- **Fog with depression or chronic inflammatory illness:** Bullmore.
- **General lifestyle and diet "reset":** Naidoo, Mosconi's *Brain Food*, or Dow. Dow's 3-week structure suits readers who want a plan.
- **Low-carb or keto-curious readers:** Perlmutter and Lugavere. Recommend these only with strong caveats, if at all.
- **Fog that persists, is new or is severe:** see a clinician first. Thyroid tests, B12, iron and ferritin, sleep apnea, depression, medications and Long COVID are common, treatable causes that books cannot diagnose. This reflects the Denno et al. 2025 view that brain fog has many causes. — [Denno et al. 2025](https://www.masudhusain.org/s/Denno-et-al-2025-TINS.pdf)
