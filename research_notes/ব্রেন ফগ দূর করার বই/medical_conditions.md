## Q1. Which books exist on long COVID brain fog / recovery (2021-2026)?

### Takeaway
The most credible long-COVID titles are *The Long Covid Self-Help Guide* (Oxford Post-Covid Clinic team, ed. Emily Fraser, 2022) and *Clearing the Fog* (Vanderbilt psychologist James C. Jackson, 2023 US / 2024 UK). Other titles exist, but they are either self-published with no independent reviews or written by authors who push supplements, so treat them with caution.

### Cited Findings

**Book 1: *The Long Covid Self-Help Guide: Practical Ways to Manage Symptoms* (2022)**
- Credited to "The Specialists from the Post-Covid Clinic, Oxford". Some library catalogues list it as "edited by Emily Fraser", and Fraser co-wrote chapter 1 and the investigations appendix. Published 2022 by Green Tree (a Bloomsbury imprint), London. About 176 pages, ISBN 9781399402026. — [Darebin Libraries catalogue](https://darebin.spydus.com/cgi-bin/spydus.exe/FULL/OPAC/BIBENQ/74967796/37553547,1); [Backstory listing](https://backstory.london/products/9781399402026)

- The clinic was set up in 2020 and was among the first UK clinics to bring together the range of doctors and therapists that Long Covid care needs. — [Charter Bookstore listing (publisher copy)](https://www.charterbookstore.com/book/9781399402026)

**What NHS services say about long-COVID brain fog (useful alongside the books)**
- NHS trust leaflets describe long-COVID cognitive symptoms as poor concentration, slower thinking, and trouble with immediate or short-term memory, word-finding and planning. - News coverage of a Lancet-linked study reports that long-COVID brain fog can last for years in some people. — [AOL/news report](https://www.aol.com/long-covid-brain-fog-last-134903660.html)

### Inferences
- For a reader-facing list, the Oxford guide (multidisciplinary NHS clinic, mainstream approach) and Jackson's book (clinical psychologist, cognitive-rehab angle, NPR coverage) are the two to recommend.
- endfatigue.com is the site of Dr Jacob Teitelbaum, who is known for supplement-heavy fatigue protocols. This comes from general knowledge, not from the fetched page, so verify it before stating it.
- Both credible books match NHS advice: pacing/energy management, cognitive strategies, and psychological support. Neither offers a cure.

### Gaps
- Full publisher pages for *Clearing the Fog* (exact US publisher and date, Jackson's full title at Vanderbilt) could not be fetched because of a network block on hachette.co.uk.
- No independent professional reviews (Kirkus, PW, BMJ) were found for any long-COVID title.
- Patient organisations (e.g., Long Covid Support) have no recommended-reading list in these results. Not checked.

## Q2. Best books on ME/CFS and fibro fog, chemo brain, and adult ADHD (incl. credibility concerns)?

### Takeaway
- **ME/CFS and fibromyalgia:** Bruce Campbell's pacing-centred book is the safest pick. Ginevra Liptan's *The FibroManual* is a well-regarded fibromyalgia guide by an MD who has the condition, though it leans toward functional medicine.
- **Myhill:** Sarah Myhill's book is widely sold but comes from a doctor who has been repeatedly suspended by the GMC. It should carry a clear warning or be left off.
- **Chemo brain:** *Your Brain After Chemo* (2009) is still the standard book, but it is dated.
- **Adult ADHD:** Hallowell & Ratey's *Driven to Distraction* (revised 2011) and *ADHD 2.0* (2021) are popular, accessible picks. Barkley's *Taking Charge of Adult ADHD* (2nd ed. 2021) is the most research-driven.

Mayo Clinic context on chemo brain:
- The mechanism is not fully understood. Contributors probably include cancer-related stress, sleep problems, pain medicines, and other conditions such as thyroid problems and nutritional deficiency.
- No medicine is approved specifically for chemo brain. Alternative treatments have not been shown to prevent or cure it, and many people improve over time.
- See a doctor if the problems are troubling you, keep a symptom journal, and say specifically if they affect your work. Doctors may order blood tests or brain scans to rule out other causes.
- Source: [Mayo Clinic: Chemo brain, symptoms & causes](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/chemo-brain/symptoms-causes/syc-20351060?p=1); [Mayo Clinic: Chemo brain](https://www.mayoclinic.org/health/chemo-brain/DS01109)

## Q3. What do medical bodies recommend? (causes of brain fog, pacing, when to see a doctor, tests)

### Takeaway
- **What it is:** Brain fog is a symptom, not a diagnosis. Causes include post-viral illness, ME/CFS, cancer treatment, thyroid disease, nutritional deficiencies (B12, iron), poor sleep, depression, anxiety and medications.
- **ME/CFS:** NICE (2021) recommends personalised energy management/pacing and advises against graded exercise therapy.
- **Chemo brain:** Mayo Clinic says no drug is approved for it.
- **When to see a doctor:** if fog persists, worsens or disrupts daily life. Sudden confusion or neurological signs need urgent care.
- **Tests:** usually basic blood tests (full blood count, thyroid, B12, ferritin/iron, glucose, sometimes vitamin D).

### Cited Findings

**ME/CFS: NICE guideline NG206 (2021)**
- Lists cognitive difficulties ("brain fog") as one of four core symptoms. People with all four core symptoms for three months or more should be referred to a specialist team. — [NICE NG206 PDF](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/resources/myalgic-encephalitis-or-encephalopathychronic-fatigue-syndrome-diagnosis-and-management-pdf-66143718094021); [iatroX summary](https://www.iatrox.com/guidelines/me-cfs)
- Energy management is a key part of a personalised management plan. It helps people stay within their energy limits so overexertion does not worsen symptoms. — [NICE NG206](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/resources/myalgic-encephalitis-or-encephalopathychronic-fatigue-syndrome-diagnosis-and-management-pdf-66143718094021)
- Any programme based on fixed incremental increases in activity, such as GET, "should not be used". Publication was paused in August 2021 over "strong views". Royal Colleges objected that GET as defined in the guideline did not reflect NHS personalised programmes. - Typical panel: full blood count, thyroid function (TSH, free T4), vitamin B12, iron studies including ferritin, vitamin D, blood glucose. — [Athens Lab blog](https://athenslab.gr/en/blog/well-being/brain-fog-symptoms-causes-diagnostic-testing-and-treatment); [Healthline](https://healthline.com/health/brain-fog)

**Red flags / when to see a doctor**
- See a doctor if fog persists, worsens or interferes with daily life. Fog that builds over days to weeks deserves a work-up, while abrupt confusion needs emergency care. — [Fox News "Ask a doctor"](https://www.foxnews.com/health/what-brain-fog-when-should-seek-medical-attention-ask-doctor)
- Worsening numbness, unsteady walking or new confusion need prompt care. — [Superpower guide](https://superpower.com/guides/how-to-fix-brain-fog)
- For chemo brain, tell your doctor if it affects your work. — [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/chemo-brain/symptoms-causes/syc-20351060?p=1)
- For ME/CFS, refer to a specialist team when core symptoms last three months or more. — [NICE NG206](https://nice.org.uk/guidance/ng206/resources/myalgic-encephalitis-or-encephalopathychronic-fatigue-syndrome-diagnosis-and-management-pdf-66143718094021)

### Inferences
- The recommended books agree with the guidelines on one common message: rule out treatable causes first (thyroid, B12, iron, sleep, medications, mood), then manage the fog with pacing/energy management and practical cognitive strategies such as lists, routines and single-tasking.
- Stroke-type warning signs belong in any red-flag list: sudden confusion, slurred speech, one-sided weakness. This is standard emergency guidance, but it was not separately sourced here, so the writer should cite NHS 111/999 guidance if including it.
