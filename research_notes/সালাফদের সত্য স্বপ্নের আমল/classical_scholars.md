## 1. Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani, Fath al-Bari, Kitab al-Ta'bir (vol. 12): true dreams as a part of prophethood, truthfulness, sahar, wudu, etiquettes

### Takeaway
Ibn Hajar, drawing on Ibn al-Arabi and al-Qurtubi, says that the dream counted as "a part of prophethood" is the good dream of a righteous, truthful believer. A disbeliever's or a sinner's dream is not counted that way. He also says that wudu before sleep makes dreams more truthful and further from the devil's play. He sums up the etiquette as three for a good dream and four for a bad one (Fath 12/370, per secondary source).

### Inferences
- Ibn Hajar treats wudu, the right side and recitation as means and recommended practices, not conditions. He also flags that some items in the list come from dream interpreters rather than from hadith. The report should distinguish what is established by hadith from what comes from scholarly experience.
- The quote "أصدقكم رؤيا أصدقكم حديثا" and Ibn Hajar's explanation of the hadith "إذا اقترب الزمان" are in vol. 12 (باب القيد في المنام). Their exact text could not be verified (see Gaps).

## 2. Al-Nawawi (Sharh Sahih Muslim), al-Qadi Iyad and al-Qurtubi (al-Mufhim): conditions and etiquettes

### Takeaway
Al-Qurtubi gives the clearest criterion: only the dream of a "truthful, righteous Muslim" counts as part of prophethood. The dream of a disbeliever, a sinner or someone of mixed conduct (مخلِّط) does not count in that sense, even if it sometimes comes true. Al-Nawawi explains that the prescribed etiquettes are a means of protection from the harm of a dream, just as charity protects wealth.

### Cited Findings
- **[Classical text: al-Qurtubi, al-Mufhim]** In meaning: «إنما يكون (تكون الرؤيا) جزءاً من النبوة للمسلم الصادق الصالح، الذي يناسب حاله حال الأنبياء، فأُكرم بنوع مما أُكرم به الأنبياء وهو الاطلاع على الغيب، وأما الكافر والفاسق والمخلِّط فلا، ولو صدقت رؤياهم أحياناً» — [IslamQA 378790 (search summary)](https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/378790?traffic_source=main_islamqa); [alukah, "أصناف الناس في الرؤيا" (search summary)](https://www.alukah.net/sharia/1001/8872/%d8%a3%d8%b5%d9%86%d8%a7%d9%81-%d8%a7%d9%84%d9%86%d8%a7%d8%b3-%d9%81%d9%8a-%d8%a7%d9%84%d8%b1%d8%a4%d9%8a%d8%a7/). A search summary places the reference in **al-Mufhim, vol. 18**. The volume number is doubtful, since editions of al-Mufhim usually have 7 volumes and al-Qurtubi's tafsir has 20, so "vol. 18" may be a confusion with something else. Check it.
  - Bengali: «স্বপ্ন নবুওয়াতের অংশ কেবল সেই সত্যবাদী নেককার মুসলিমের জন্য, যার অবস্থা নবীদের অবস্থার সাথে সামঞ্জস্যপূর্ণ; ফলে নবীদের যা দিয়ে সম্মানিত করা হয়েছিল—গায়েবের কিছু অবগতি—তার এক প্রকার দিয়ে তাকে সম্মানিত করা হয়। কাফির, ফাসিক ও মিশ্র-আমলকারীর ক্ষেত্রে নয়, যদিও কখনো তাদের স্বপ্ন সত্য হয়।»

## 4. Ibn al-Qayyim: Madarij al-Salikin, I'lam al-Muwaqqi'in, al-Ruh, Zad al-Ma'ad

### Takeaway
In Madarij al-Salikin, under «المرتبة العاشرة من مراتب الهداية: الرؤيا الصادقة» (vol. 1), Ibn al-Qayyim gives the most complete classical list of means for true dreams: truthfulness in speech, halal food, guarding commands and prohibitions, sleeping in full purity facing the qibla, and dhikr until sleep. He calls dreams at sahar the most truthful. He divides dreams into three kinds (rahmani, nafsani, shaytani). He says the dream of a prophet is revelation, while anyone else's dream is weighed against explicit revelation: act on it if it agrees, otherwise do not.

### Cited Findings

## 5. Ibn Taymiyya, Majmu' al-Fatawa: dreams and their limits

### Takeaway
Ibn Taymiyya says that a bare dream with no evidence for its soundness cannot establish anything, by agreement of the scholars (27/457, per secondary source). Isra'iliyyat and dreams cannot establish a shar'i ruling, whether recommendation or anything else. Many Sufis build on dreams and inner experiences that they take to be kashf, and these are illusions.

### Cited Findings
- **[Classical text: Majmu' al-Fatawa 27/457, per secondary source]** «الرؤيا المحضة التي لا دليل على صحتها لا يجوز أن يُثبت بها شيء بالاتفاق» — Bengali: «যে নিছক স্বপ্নের বিশুদ্ধতার পক্ষে কোনো দলিল নেই, তা দিয়ে কোনো কিছু সাব্যস্ত করা সর্বসম্মতভাবে জায়েয নয়।» — [binbaz.org.sa "الرؤى والمنامات" category / al-Jazirah (search summary)](https://binbaz.org.sa/categories/objective/383); [al-Jazirah, "الرؤى والمنامات" (2007)](https://www.al-jazirah.com/2007/20071116/is4.htm).

## 6. Ibn Rajab al-Hanbali and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr (al-Tamhid, Malik's view)

### Takeaway
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports in al-Tamhid (1/288, Moroccan Ministry of Awqaf edition, per secondary source) that Malik was asked, "Can anyone interpret dreams?" He answered, «أبالنبوة يُلعب؟» ("Is prophethood to be played with?"). Only someone skilled should interpret. If he sees good he should tell it; if he sees something disliked he should say something good or stay silent. A dream should not be interpreted as good while the interpreter thinks it bad, «الرؤيا جزء من النبوة فلا يُتلاعب بالنبوة». I found nothing from Ibn Rajab.

## 7. Al-Shatibi (al-I'tisam / al-Muwafaqat): dreams cannot establish rulings

### Takeaway
In al-I'tisam, al-Shatibi counts among the weakest arguments those who take their practice from dreams and abandon the shar'i rulings. He says the dream of anyone other than a prophet is never a basis for a shar'i ruling. It must be weighed against the established rulings: if they allow it, one may act on it; otherwise it must be abandoned. Its benefit is only glad tidings or warning.

### Cited Findings
- **[Classical text: al-I'tisam, via secondary sites]** In meaning: «الرؤيا من غير الأنبياء لا يُحكم بها شرعاً على حال، إلا أن تُعرض على ما في أيدينا من الأحكام الشرعية، فإن سوَّغتها عُمل بمقتضاها، وإلا وجب تركها والإعراض عنها، وإنما فائدتها البشارة أو النذارة خاصة، وأما استفادة الأحكام فلا» — Bengali: «নবী ছাড়া অন্য কারও স্বপ্ন দিয়ে কোনো অবস্থাতেই শরঈ বিধান দেওয়া হয় না; বরং আমাদের কাছে থাকা শরঈ বিধানের সামনে পেশ করা হবে—অনুমোদন করলে তদনুযায়ী আমল, নইলে তা পরিত্যাগ করা ওয়াজিব। এর উপকারিতা কেবল সুসংবাদ বা সতর্কবার্তা; বিধান গ্রহণ নয়।» — [dd-sunnah.net, "مقتطفات من الاعتصام" (secondary)](https://www.dd-sunnah.net/forum/archive/index.php/t-98802.html); [alhesn.net, "الرؤيا ليست حجة شرعية"](https://www.alhesn.net/play/14468).

## 8. "Ta'bir al-Ru'ya" / "Muntakhab al-Kalam fi Tafsir al-Ahlam" attributed to Ibn Sirin: authenticity and caution

### Takeaway
Modern researchers and fatwa centres agree that the attribution of the circulating book to Ibn Sirin is not established. Al-Dhahabi and Ibn Kathir do not list it among his works. Recent editors (Bilal Orfali and Lina Jammal) argue from the introduction and the copyist's notes that the book is actually by Abu Sa'd al-Kharkushi. Islamweb's position is that even if the attribution were sound, the book cannot be relied on alone, because interpretation changes with person, time and place.

### Cited Findings
- **[Modern summary]** The attribution has not been established with certainty. Imams such as al-Dhahabi and Ibn Kathir did not list this book among his works — [Islamweb fatwa 25645, "نبذة عن ابن سيرين ومدى صحة نسبة كتاب تفسير الأحلام إليه"](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/25645/).
- **[Modern summary]** Even if the attribution were sound, the book should not be relied on alone, since interpretation varies with the state of the dreamer, time and place. It may be consulted alongside other works — [Islamweb fatwa 41552, "حكم الاعتماد في تفسير الأحلام على كتاب ابن سيرين"](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/41552/).
- **[Modern academic view]** The editors Dr. Bilal Orfali and Dr. Lina Jammal hold that the book is correctly attributed to Abu Sa'd al-Kharkushi (d. 407 AH / 1016 CE), based on its introduction and the copyist's wording. Popular editions are full of errors — [al-Modon, "تفسير الأحلام لابن سيرين... والأخطاء الشائعة" (2023)](https://www.almodon.com/culture/2023/05/12/%D8%AA%D9%81%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AD%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%A8%D9%86-%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%86-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%AE%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%A1-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B4%D8%A7%D8%A6%D8%B9%D8%A9).
- **[Modern view, social media]** Ibn Sirin interpreted dreams orally. Later compilers gathered these reports centuries afterwards (probably between the 7th and 9th centuries AH), and al-Khalil ibn Shahin al-Zahiri was among them — [Salah al-Ansari on X](https://x.com/salah_alansari/status/1909331353834749983?lang=ar). ## 9. Practical list of means (asbab) for true dreams, and the three kinds of dream

### Takeaway
Classical sources plus the hadith give this set of means. **(a) Established by hadith:** truthfulness in speech (Muslim), wudu before sleep and lying on the right side (al-Bara''s hadith), the etiquette for good and bad dreams. **(b) From scholarly experience and reasoning (Ibn al-Qayyim, Ibn Hajar):** halal food, guarding commands and prohibitions, facing the qibla, dhikr until sleep, the sahar timing (the hadith itself is weak, but Ibn al-Qayyim gives the reason), avoiding sins and mixed conduct (al-Qurtubi). Three kinds of dream: rahmani (glad tidings from Allah), nafsani (the self's own talk), shaytani (the devil's grief and play).

### Cited Findings
- Truthfulness, halal food, guarding commands and prohibitions, full purity, facing the qibla, dhikr; sahar dreams are most truthful — Ibn al-Qayyim, Madarij vol. 1 — [Islamweb library](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/library/content/119/27/); [al-Eqtisadiah](https://www.aleqt.com/2009/05/01/article_161163.html).
- Wudu makes dreams more truthful and further from the devil's play — Ibn Hajar — [Islamweb fatwa 26613](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/26613/).
- Truthfulness, purity, the right side and recitation of certain surahs (some of these come from dream interpreters, not marfu' hadith) — Ibn Hajar via [Islamweb fatwa 63233](https://www.islamweb.org/ar/fatwa/63233/).
- Being a truthful, righteous Muslim; the sinner and the person of mixed conduct are excluded — al-Qurtubi — [IslamQA 378790](https://islamqa.info/ar/answers/378790?traffic_source=main_islamqa).
- Three kinds (rahmani / nafsani / shaytani) — Ibn al-Qayyim, based on the hadith — [Madarij (islamicbook.ws)](https://www.islamicbook.ws/amma/mdarj-alsalkin-001.html).
- Etiquette for good dreams (3) and disliked dreams (4) — Fath 12/370 — [itihad.org](https://www.itihad.org/29998/%D9%87%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%82%D8%B1%D9%8A%D9%86%D8%A9-%D9%85%D9%88%D8%AC%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A9-%D8%A3%D9%85-%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%9F/).
- **[Modern summary]** Scholars regarded true dreams as an early glad tiding for the awliya' and linked them to taqwa and righteousness, but as signs that raise the likelihood, not certainties — [mawdoo3 (non-academic)](https://mawdoo3.com/متى_تكون_الرؤيا_صادقة).

### Inferences
- The question also lists "avoiding overeating" and "sleeping early". No classical text supporting either came up in the search results. They may be modern additions, so they should go in the report only as general advice, not as classical statements.

### Gaps

## 10. Contemporary scholarly summaries (secondary aids only)

### Takeaway
Modern fatwa centres (Islamweb, IslamQA, binbaz.org.sa, the Egyptian Dar al-Ifta) restate the classical texts above: purity is not a condition but a cause of truthful dreams, dreams do not establish rulings, interpretation is only for the skilled, and the book attributed to Ibn Sirin is unreliable.

- [Islamweb 104211](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/104211/): purity is not a condition, but brings one closer to a true dream.
- [Islamweb 191129, "الرؤيا لا يثبت بها حكم ولا أمر ولا تحديد عمر"](https://www.islamweb.net/ar/fatwa/191129/): a dream establishes no ruling, command or lifespan (title only seen).
